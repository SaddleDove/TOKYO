# CHAPTER 12 — CURFEW

**All · 24 December 2024 · TOKYO**

---

### THEO

Christmas in Tokyo is a marketing decision. There are lights on the wires and a fake tree outside the station and couples in coats that cost more than my rent, walking very close and looking at each other like they have solved something.

In the club there is no tree. There is a scaffolding stage and a wet wall and Mei has put one string of lights around the mirror, which is the closest thing to decoration this room has ever had. She says it is for the customers. I know it is for us.

I did the song. No cardboard officer. No visa joke. No mention of the landlord. I did a song my mother used to play while ironing, and I did not introduce it, and I did not explain it, and halfway through I forgot the words in English and sang them in a language I do not speak, and the room sang along anyway, badly, in six languages, none of them the one I started in.

At 2:58 I was still on stage. At 2:59 I was still on stage. At three the lights came on and I stopped, which is a new thing for me. I have always finished the gesture. Tonight I didn't. The light hit the glue on my face and I stood there in front of seventy-one people with my hands down.

Nobody moved.

That's the thing. Nobody moved.

Usually the room empties like a lung. Coats, goodbyes, the small negotiations of who is sober enough to take the train. Tonight seventy-one people stood in their places under fluorescent light, blinking, looking old and tired and real, and nobody reached for a coat.

Mei was behind the bar with her hands flat on the counter. Rin was by the sink with one hand on the small recorder. Yusuf was by the wall with both hands out of his pockets for once. Cass had the camera on the table and her hands nowhere near it. Even the old man in row three was still sitting there with his untouched drink.

At 3:04 somebody coughed. It was the cat.

I have never been so grateful for a cat.

---

### RIN

The lights come on and nobody goes.

This is not a fact. It is a weather event. A thing that happens to a room and not to a person.

My hands are on the recorder. I should put it in my bag. I do not. The tape is finished. The voice has said the sentence twice tonight and the room has heard it a hundred times and I am the only person who keeps the tape in a box instead of throwing it away, which is the reason I keep a lot of things past their date.

Theo is on the stage with the light on his face. He looks younger and older at the same time. This is what happens when glue dries.

Mei has not moved behind the bar. This is how I know something is wrong. Mei always moves. Hands, glasses, rag, hands. When Mei stops, the whole room stops with her.

At 3:06 a woman near the stairs starts to sing. Quietly. Not a performance. A person standing in a room with the lights on and singing because the alternative is to leave. Someone else joins. Then Yusuf, from the wall, in English that is almost correct, and I know the song, because I know all the songs in this room now, which is a thing I did not plan.

I do not sing. I put the recorder in my bag. Then I take it out again.

Outside, the rain has stopped. It has been raining since November and tonight it has stopped for eleven minutes and nobody has said anything about it, which is strange, because weather is what we talk about when we cannot talk.

Theo says, "Well."

Mei says, "Well."

Nobody goes.

---

### MEI

At three exactly the lights come on and nobody leaves.

I have worked eleven years in this room. I have seen a man propose to a woman at 2:59 and be told *ask me at five* and finish his drink alone. I have seen a queen fall off the scaffolding and get up and finish the number because falling is not the same as stopping. I have seen a man cry into a glass and then pay for the glass, which is a thing you can only do in a country where crying has a price.

I have never seen this.

Seventy-one people under fluorescent light. One cat. No coats. No goodbyes. Nobody counting. Theo said no visa joke and somebody should tell the room, because the room is behaving like a man who has forgotten he has a deadline.

My hands are flat on the counter. The rag is under them. Hands don't lie, and mine are saying something I do not want to hear, which is: *let them stay*.

Let them stay. Four more minutes. Four minutes is nothing. Four minutes is a cigarette. Four minutes is a room that does not have to close yet because nobody has told it to.

I think about a notice in the drawer. April first. I think about the man in the suit and the man with the wedding ring on the wrong hand and the money coming out of an account with a name like a small grey animal on the twenty-eighth of every month, and I think: *I am paying to keep a door open that nobody will let me lock.*

At 3:12 I do the thing. I go to the stereo and I put the bass back on, quietly, so low you feel it in your teeth before you hear it. Not a song. Just the low part. A room needs a heartbeat.

Theo looks at me from the stage. I do not look back. If I look back he will smile and then it becomes a moment, and a moment is a thing you can sell, and I am not selling tonight.

財務大臣 jumps onto the bar and knocks over a glass. It breaks. Nobody moves to clean it up. Fine. The cat can have the glass too.

At 3:20 people start to go. Slowly. In ones and twos. Not because the music stopped — I leave it on — but because a room can only hold a thing for so long before the thing becomes too heavy to stand under.

By 3:38 there is nobody. Theo is still on the stage. Rin is still by the sink. Yusuf is still by the wall. Cass is at the table with her hands in her lap.

I wipe the counter. I write one line in the ledger, and then I close it.

*2024 — nobody left.*

---

### YUSUF

At three the lights come on and nobody goes.

I do not understand this. In my head, three means go. Three is a rule. Steel goes where steel goes, three means go, the call finishes at forty minutes, the building is forty-seven floors. Rules are the shape of the world. A person can be lost in a city but not in a rule.

Tonight the rule is broken and nobody is afraid.

I stand by the wall with my hands out of my pockets. This is new. Iqbal used to say *you make a cage in your head and then you live in it*, and tonight I have my hands out of my pockets and I have not made a cage, and it is Christmas, which is a day my mother does not celebrate and Aldi celebrates because his mother likes the lights.

At 3:06 somebody sings. I know the song. It is the song Iqbal sang at 6:40 on Friday with his hands on the beam. I sing it in English I do not know, and I get the words wrong, and a woman next to me sings the Japanese words, and Theo sings something else entirely, and all three of us are correct.

Then Rin looks at me. She does not smile. She looks at me the way a person looks at a thing that is standing when they expected it to be on the floor. I put my hands up a little. Not dancing. Just up.

Theo sees and makes a face like I have just won a prize on a television show, and I laugh, which is wrong, because it is Christmas and a man died last week and my boyfriend is in Bekasi and my hands have cement on them and the room has seventy-one people in it and no permit.

But I laugh. It comes out. I do not stop it.

Outside, the rain has stopped for eleven minutes. I say *otsukaresama* to the door on the way out, which is wrong, I know it is wrong, but the door has been working hard tonight.

---

### CASS

The camera is on the table and my hands are in my lap. This is a sentence I have never been able to write before tonight.

At three the lights come on and nobody leaves. A room under fluorescent light with seventy-one people in it who have all agreed, silently, to pretend that the light is not a light but a kind of weather. I want to film this. I want to film it so badly my fingers are making small movements on my thighs, the way they do when I am holding a thing that isn't there.

The grant report wants this. *Subject refuses closure. Community constitutes itself against temporal regulation.* I can write the sentence. I can write it in my head while I sit here with my hands in my lap. That is the horror of it: the sentence is already written, even though I did not film it.

I have been in this room for four hours with my eyes and no machine, and I have discovered that I do not know how to look. I keep reaching for a frame. I keep arranging the light in my head. I keep thinking about where the old man in row three would sit in the edit.

At 3:06 someone sings. I do not know the song. Three people are singing it in three languages, none of which they fully speak, and the effect is not a chorus, it is a small collapse. I watch the way Theo's mouth moves when he stops trying to be funny. I watch Rin watching Yusuf's hands. I watch Mei behind the bar with her hands flat on the counter, which is the most shot-like thing I have ever seen and which I cannot shoot.

This is what I have instead: a memory of a room, which will degrade. A recording that is already editing itself into a story about me.

The old man in row three gets up at 3:31 and leaves four thousand yen on the table. I look at the money. I do not film it. I want to know whose son he is. I want to know who he was before the taxi. I want to know everything, which is the hunger, and the hunger is the problem, and the problem is me.

On the street at 3:40, my hands are still in my pockets. I have not taken out the camera. I am twenty-six years old and I am walking through Shibuya at 3:40 in the morning with nothing to show for it, and I have never been so frightened in my life.

---

### KENJI

Rain stopped. 3:02 a.m.

I am parked on Maruyamacho. Engine off. Meter off. This is different. A taxi with the meter off is a room with nobody in it.

The music comes through the wall. Quiet. Then quieter. Then a different sound — bass only, very low, coming through the ground.

I know this sound. I do not know where I know it from.

At 3:20 people come out. They stand on the street. They do not go home. This is strange. People at three go home. This is the whole point of three.

A tall man comes out and stands under the awning. No sunglasses. No wig. He looks at the laundromat like a man looking at a place he has been thrown out of, or a place he has been let into, and I cannot tell which, because his face is doing both.

A small woman comes out behind him. Grey sweatshirt. Wet hair. She sees my car. I do not move. She looks at the window, and at the licence, and then at the dryer, and she goes back inside.

I have driven eleven years. I know when a person has seen me and decided I am a taxi, and when a person has seen me and decided I am a man. This one decided both and stayed with the second.

At 3:41 the music stops. The street becomes a street. The cleaning truck comes, slowly, brushing the gutters. I watch it pass.

My rating is 4.8. One review says: *driver kept crying*. Tonight I have not cried. This is new also.

Meter: zero yen. A meter at zero is a strange thing. It is waiting. It has no number yet.

I sit in the car with the heat off and the windows down a finger's width, and I listen to a room that is not there, and I do not go in.

I have never gone in.

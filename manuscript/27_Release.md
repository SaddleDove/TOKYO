# CHAPTER 27 — RELEASE

**Mei · 28 February 2025 · 梅屋 / hospital**

Two weeks ago they let me out at 9:40 on Saturday with a paper and a cough and no apology. This is a country that does not apologise. It *cooperates*. It gives you a number and a word that means *henceforth*, and you put them in your pocket next to nothing and you go home.

Home is 梅屋, which is a failing bar that opens at three and closes at six and has three customers, one of whom is hiding from his wife. On Friday there are two. The tourist who photographs the jar of broken openers has gone, which is a mercy, and the man who sleeps has been replaced by a woman who drinks alone and watches the door, which is the same customer in a different coat.

"Good morning," I said.

"Is it morning?" she said.

"No." I poured. "It is a bar. Time is a customer. It can come in whenever it wants."

---

My father is in a room on the third floor of a hospital in Nakano with a view of another building. He has one shoe on at home and no shoes here, and he keeps asking the nurse for his cardigan, which is in the bath, in the water, where I put it back because he told me not to take it, because he is a man who has decided that the wet thing is his and nobody can touch it.

I go every day at 4:30, after the bar, before the club. I shave his head with a razor and I do not take the cardigan. He holds still for me. He has started saying thank you in Japanese, which he never used to say, because Japanese was for customers.

On Friday he called me by my name.

"Mei Ling," he said.

"Yes."

"Where is my sister."

"I am here."

"No." He looked at me with his own eyes, which are still his eyes, and he said something in Taiwanese about a door, and then the door went somewhere else in his head and he was gone for twenty minutes.

He came back. He said, "Have you eaten?"

"I ate."

"Don't lie to your mother."

"She is dead."

"Then lie to me."

We looked at each other. He laughed. It is the first time he has laughed in four years and it is because I said something true and he decided it was a joke, which is the way most things are said in this family.

---

A notice says 1 April, 8 a.m. I put it on the bar and read it again on Monday at 4 p.m. with the baseball on the television and the woman watching the door.

The twentieth is thirty-four days from the raid. Ninety-two days from when the son first put an envelope on this bar. I have been counting since November and I have lost count twice and started again, which is a thing people do with dates that matter.

Theo came in at 5 with a cold that has lasted two months and a piece of paper in his hand. The receipt from the immigration office. February 28. Fifteen days from the show.

"I need a letter from my landlord," he said.

"I am not your landlord."

"Yes."

"The man who texts you photographs of the leak is your landlord."

"Yes."

"Ask him."

"He says the leak is my fault."

"Then fix the leak."

"I am a drag queen."

"So fix the leak."

He put his head on the bar, which is a thing he does, and I put a glass of ginger beside it, which is a thing I do.

"ORIGINS," he said, into the bar. "The last Saturday. March fifteenth."

"Yes."

"No cameras. No phones. Forty-eight people."

"Seventy-one."

"Forty-eight on the permit."

"Seventy-one in the room." I wiped the counter. "This is the last one. Do it properly."

He lifted his head. His eye makeup from last night was still on, which he had not washed off, which is a thing people do when they have no mirror at home that isn't also a sink.

"Properly how?"

"You know how."

"No. Say it."

I do not say things like this. I do not say *you were good* or *I am proud* or *this mattered*, because those are sentences that belong to a mother, and I have had a mother and I have a father who calls me sister and I know what sentences can do to a person.

But the man is lying on my bar with last night's eyeliner on his face and today's appointment about a form behind him, and the room is closing on the twentieth, and the voice in the wall is a man I have known for eleven years without knowing his name, and my father is upstairs in a hospital asking where his sister is.

"Like you mean it," I said. "That's how."

He looked at me. Then he said, "Right."

"Right."

"Properly."

"Yes."

He sat up and drank the ginger. Then he said, "What if I forget and do the joke?"

"Then I will throw something."

"You have forty-seven broken openers."

"I have excellent aim."

He laughed. I laughed. This is the last thing I want to say about this conversation: it was funny, and it was the truest thing either of us said all month, and neither of us has repeated it, which is why it is still here.

---

At 23:00 I open. Rain. The password is about a house. I do not know who picks these. I suspect Rin, who has a face like a person who knows everything and has decided not to say, but she denies it every time I ask, which is a denial with too much detail, which is a thing that means yes.

The room was full. The cat was where he always was. The old man in row three moves his glass a finger's width to the left. At 00:17 the voice comes on and the room keeps dancing.

I stand behind the bar and watch hands.

Yusuf's hands: cement, always, but he has stopped touching the wall. Rin's hands: on the cassette player, then off, then on. Cass's hands: in her lap. Theo's hands: still, before the show, for the first time since I met him.

My hands: fine.

My hands are fine. This is the least interesting thing about me.

At 3:12 I take the ledger out and open page 214. The last lines:

*2025.02.14 — a bottle broke.*
*April — a date.*

Under them I write one line. I write it slowly, because a line in this book is a bill, and I am the one who pays it, and I have paid it every month on the twenty-eighth for ten years, and this is the last one I will pay in this room.

*March 15 — one show. No cameras.*

Then I close the book and put it back behind the bottles nobody orders, and I turn off the television, which is showing baseball in February because the television has never once cared about months.

Ninety-two days is a long time if you are counting forward from a door closing. It is a very short time if you are counting backward from a room that has to be emptied.

I have never been good at arithmetic. I am excellent at doors.

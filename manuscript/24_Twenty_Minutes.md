# CHAPTER 24 — TWENTY MINUTES

**Mei · 14 February 2025 · TOKYO**

Valentine's Day in this country is a thing women buy chocolate for men they do not like, which is a custom I have never understood and which I have no intention of understanding at fifty.

In 梅屋 at 5 p.m. there were three customers and one of them was hiding from his wife and one of them was asleep and one of them was a tourist taking photographs of the jar of broken openers. The tourist asked me if I was alone. I said yes. He said, *you should be with someone today*. I said, *I am with forty-seven openers, all of them useless, all of them mine*. He took a photograph of the jar and left.

At 9:40 a woman from the group chat sent a message. Four words. *They got a tip.*

At 9:41 another. *Twenty minutes.*

Twenty minutes is a short time and a long time. In twenty minutes you can empty a room of seventy-one people and one cat, or you can stand behind a bar and watch hands and do neither.

I did not call anyone. There is no phone tree in this club. This is not an accident. A club with a phone tree is a club with a list, and a list is a thing the police can take, and I have been in a country sixteen years and I know what a list is.

I went down the stairs. Dryer four. The password was *a letter does not arrive if nobody sends it*, which is a sentence someone in the group chat wrote at 9:43 and which is too long for a password and which nobody said correctly, and Momo let everybody in anyway, because he could see my face and my face said *twenty minutes*.

---

At 2:39 the bass stopped. I did not stop it. The DJ stopped it, because he saw the same thing I saw through the crack in the door at the top of the stairs: a torch, moving in the laundromat.

Twenty minutes had become seventeen. Or nine. Time does this when a room is in trouble. It does not go forward. It goes sideways.

"The alley," I said.

I did not shout. I did not need to. I have worked eleven years behind this bar and I have one voice for customers and one voice for emergencies and they are almost the same voice, which is why people obey the second one.

Theo came off the scaffolding. Rin's hand came off the cassette player. Yusuf moved away from the wall. Cass put the camera in her bag without being told, which I noticed, and which I put in the ledger later, in my head, because there was no time for a real book.

"The alley. Now. No phones. Walk, don't run. Momo, front."

Momo, who never speaks, nodded once and opened the door behind the bar that goes to the alley, which is not on any plan of this building and which I put in myself in 2016 with a crowbar and a man who was paid in drinks.

Seventy-one people went through a door two metres wide in four minutes. This is a thing I did not think was possible and which is possible when a room understands the word *go*.

---

The cat was not going.

財務大臣 was on the bar, sitting on a clean glass with his whole fat grey body, and when I reached for him he hissed, which he has never done in his life.

"Cat," I said.

Theo came back from the alley door. "I can't believe this cat."

"Take the cat."

"I'm scared of the cat."

"Take the goddamn cat."

He picked up the cat, and the cat put all four feet into his chest and screamed, which is a sound I have never heard from a cat and which I will hear for the rest of my life, and the cat knocked over a bottle of something and the bottle broke and somebody swore in Tagalog.

This took eight minutes.

Eight minutes is a long time. In eight minutes a room can empty, and a room can fill with police, and a room can do both at once, which is what happened, because at 2:52 the torch came through the stairs and at 2:53 three officers were in the room and at 2:54 they were looking at the broken bottle and a cat who was still screaming in the arms of a very tall man in a wig who had forgotten to take the wig off.

I put the night in the ledger. I wrote only what happened. The cat. The bottle. The time. The rest I left out, which is how I have survived eleven years behind this bar.

The cat went home that night too. He did not know he had done anything. This is the whole difference between a cat and a woman.

---

They asked for the owner.

I said, "I am the bartender."

"Owner."

"I am the bartender."

A woman officer looked at the bar. At the bottles. At the sink with three metres of stainless steel and cups drying on the rack. At the ledger, which was not on the bar, which was behind the bottles nobody orders, which she did not find.

"Name."

"Mei Ling."

"Not here," she said. This is a sentence said by people who have decided you have a name that belongs somewhere else.

"Here is my name," I said. "Not here is my mother's name. Different thing."

She wrote something. The pen was like Theo's pen. Cheap. Presses too hard.

They checked the fire exits. One. They checked the capacity. Forty-eight. They counted the people. One.

Me.

"Everybody left," she said.

"At three," I said. "You are early."

She looked at the clock. It said 2:57.

At three exactly the lights came on. All of them. No warning, no countdown. One face gets ten years older in one second, and my face is the only one in the room, so there is nobody to compare it to, which is a particular kind of loneliness I had not expected.

The glue on the stage cracked. A dry field. Theo's wig was on the floor by the alley door, left behind in the rush, and under the fluorescent light it looked like a small dead animal.

I picked it up. I put it on the stool in the mirror room. I do not know why I did this. I have never done anything like it in my life.

---

They took the ledger.

No — they did not. I want to be exact, because exactness is the only thing I have left tonight. They took the *book* from behind the bar, which is a different book, a book with money in it, with numbers and dates and a bank account with a name like a small grey animal. They did not take the other one, which is under the sink behind the ginger, which I put there with my left hand while the woman officer was writing with hers.

Money is easy to take. A ledger of who showed up is not. You have to know it exists.

They left at 3:31. They did not arrest me. They wrote a number on a paper and gave me the number and said a word that means *cooperation* and a word that means *henceforth*, and the second word is much heavier than the first.

I sat on a stool behind the bar. The room was very quiet. A room with seventy-one people in it is loud. A room with one person in it is a different room entirely, and it is the same room.

At 3:40 I took out the real ledger and opened page 214. The last line was *April — a date*. Under it I wrote:

*2025.02.14 — a bottle broke.*

Then I closed it and put it back behind the ginger, and I washed the glasses, all of them, one by one, and I did not look at the clock, because the clock and I have an understanding and the understanding is over.

At 4:10 I turned off the lights. Not at three. Later. For once, later.

This is the first rule I have ever broken in my own club, and I broke it alone, and nobody clapped.

財務大臣 came out from under the speakers and sat on the stool beside me and put one paw on my forearm. Good cat.

Fine. The cat can have the last word too.

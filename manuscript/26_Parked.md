# CHAPTER 26 — PARKED

**Kenji · 15 February 2025 · Maruyamacho**

Rain. Saturday. 2:03 a.m.

I park. Engine off. Meter off. This is the third night.

The laundromat is open. A woman inside folds towels. She sees me through the glass and I know that she sees me, because she looks once and then she looks again, which is a thing people do when a thing is familiar and not explained.

I do not get out.

---

My son called at 1:30. I did not answer.

He called again at 1:34. Then a message.

*Eight p.m. Tuesday. Station. I made rice.*

I read it three times. I put the phone face down on the passenger seat, next to the blue tie, which is where I keep it now, in the glove compartment, with the map and the wipes.

The tie is in the glove compartment. I did not go.

I have never gone.

---

At 2:11 a woman comes out of the club into the alley. Grey sweatshirt. Wet hair. Small. She stands under the broken light with a cat at her feet.

She looks at my car. I know that she looks at my car. A person can look at a car in two ways: at the number, or at the man in it. She is looking at the man.

I do not move.

She says something. I cannot hear it through the window and the rain. Her mouth moves. The cat sits.

This is the third time. On Christmas she looked and went back inside. Tonight she stands there for nine minutes.

Nine minutes with the meter off is a strange thing. A meter at zero is a thing that is waiting. A man at zero is the same.

The voice comes on inside. I know it is on because the wall changes. I cannot hear the words from here, only the shape of them, which comes through concrete better than through air and which I do not name.

She puts her hand on the wall of the building. Not on the door. On the wall. Then she wipes her face with her sleeve and goes back inside.

The cat stays. The cat looks at my car for a while and then goes in after her.


---

My rating is 4.8. One review. I have read it every month for three years, and tonight I thought about writing a reply, which I would not do, because a reply is a thing a person writes when they want to be known, and I do not want to be known, I want to be called something else by one person, which is a different thing and which I will not ask for.

The son said *mother*. Twice. I did not correct him.

She said the word to a taxi tonight. I could not hear it. I know what it was, because I know the shape of a person's mouth saying a thing to a car they think is empty.

I have a tie in the glove compartment. I have a voice in a wall. I have a daughter standing in an alley saying a word to a man who will not open the window.

I do not open the window.

At three the music stops. The lights come on somewhere I cannot see. The street becomes a street again. The cleaning truck comes and brushes the gutters. I watch it pass.

I put the heat on. Then I turn it off. A man who does not sleep does not need heat.

Meter: zero.

I sit here until 3:40. Then I start the car and drive, because a taxi with the meter off is a room with nobody in it, and I have sat in that room long enough.

At 3:44 the meter moves. 180 yen. 240. 320.

A number is a thing you can trust. It starts at zero and it goes up and it does not care what you did before.

I drive until morning. No passengers. This is allowed. This is what I do.

I have never gone in.

# CHAPTER 32 — GLOVE COMPARTMENT

**Kenji · 14 March 2025 · Shibuya**

Rain. Friday. 2:10 a.m.

She gets out. The door shuts. The car is a room with one person in it again, and one person in a room is a different room entirely.

The glove compartment holds the 2011 map and the unused wipes. It closes with a small sound, like a mouth. The blue tie is gone. She took it.

I take the black glasses out of the glove compartment and put them on the seat, then take them into WASH & GO and put them on the counter. Mrs. Saito does not ask. She puts them in a paper bag.

I sit.

---

She asked me if I was a woman.

I said yes.

This is the first time I have said it. Twenty-five years. One word. In a taxi. Meter 1,240. She got out. Rain.

I did not say it to my wife. She knew from the third year. She never said. I never said.

My son called me mother. Twice. I did not correct him.

I said it to a woman. Wet hair. Screwdriver. Tape on a board. A name I carved with a key in 1999. English. Badly.

---

At 3:04 a man gets in the back. Drunk. Foreign. Smells of gin.

"Shibuya," he says.

"We are in Shibuya."

He laughs for a long time. Then he says, "Dryer four."

"I do not know."

"You know."

He pays too much. He says keep it. He gets out and goes into the laundromat, and behind the counter a woman looks up and does not smile and says something, and he nods like a man being told where left is.

This is the same man. Or a different man. All foreign men are the same man in my car at 3 a.m., and all of them are going to a room I have never entered.

Meter: 1,240 yen.

---

I drive until morning. No passengers after that. This is allowed. This is what I do.

At 8 a.m. my son is at the station. He said eight p.m. Tuesday. That Tuesday was a long time ago. I did not go. I have never gone.

He made rice. His mother asked him to ask me. She knows where I am. Everyone knows where I am.

At 8:12 a.m. I drive past the station. I see a boy in a coat his mother bought standing under the awning with a bag. He is nineteen. He has my hands, which he does not know.

I do not stop.

The blue tie is in a woman's room on the other side of Shibuya. The black glasses are in a paper bag on a laundromat counter. I have a voice in a wall and a daughter who plays it to seventy-one people and did not know, and I gave it back to her and did not say, and she asked me why and I said *you were nine*, which is the only true thing I have ever offered anyone, and she took it, which is more than I deserved.

The meter goes up. 980 yen. 1,120. 1,180. 1,240.

A number is a thing you can trust. It starts at zero and it goes up and it does not care what you did in 1999.

At 9:03 a.m. the money moves out of an account with a name like a small grey animal into the account for a room that will be empty in six days. I know about the money. I have known for four years. I do not know why I know. A man who drives at night hears things through walls.

I keep driving.

The rain stops. It has been falling since November. It has been falling since March. It has been falling my whole life, and nobody has ever once told it to stop, and nobody ever will.

Meter: 1,240 yen.

I have said it. I said it to her. I said *yes*.

I drive.
